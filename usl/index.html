Unmanned Systems Lab

We work on learning, teaming, and control for unmanned aircraft that operate alongside human partners. The lab designs its own airframes, flight control boards, software, AI/ML algorithms, and human–AI teaming strategies, and tests them outdoors so that results hold up in real-world deployment.

Research

What we work on

We study how unmanned systems learn, decide, collaborate, and stay safe, and we validate the answers on aircraft we build in the lab.

01

Human-guided learning and human–AI teaming

Autonomous systems should reduce an operator’s workload, not add to it, and they should learn what people actually want. We develop methods that learn from human ratings and preferences rather than hand-designed rewards, including rating-based reinforcement learning, multi-task reward learning from human ratings, and preference-based learning for robot behavior. A second line of work models teammates and opponents, from transformer-based agent modeling to retrieval-augmented language models for ad hoc teaming, so that an autonomous agent can coordinate with partners it has not trained with.

  • rating-based RL
  • preference-based learning
  • reward learning
  • human–AI teaming
  • agent modeling
  • LLM agents

02

Fair, interpretable, and aligned reinforcement learning

Learned policies have to be fair to every objective and stakeholder, understandable to the people who deploy them, and robust once fielded. We study fair policy learning in multi-objective and cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning, with applications such as traffic control; interpretable reinforcement learning through symbolic policy distillation and model explanations; inference-time policy alignment and multi-criterion alignment of language models; and the adaptation of vision-language-action models when a robot’s body differs from the one they were trained on. Related work covers learning from failure under sparse rewards and event-triggered reinforcement learning for control.

  • multi-objective RL
  • fairness in RL
  • multi-agent RL
  • interpretable RL
  • inference-time alignment
  • VLA models

03

Autonomy, guidance, control, and multi-agent coordination

The lab has a long record in distributed coordination, consensus, containment, and event-triggered control. Current work is in safety-critical control under input constraints, safety certificates, and energy-efficient flexible leader–follower formation.

  • autonomy
  • nonlinear guidance
  • formation control
  • event-triggered control
  • safety-critical control

04

Deployment and technology transition

We focus on deployment of learning, autonomy, and control models and algorithms on hardware in real-world environments. We build our own small unmanned aircraft, from the airframe to the flight control board, onboard computing, software, and sensing, so new methods go straight from analysis to outdoor flight tests on hardware we fully control. We build the benchmarks, datasets, and test protocols that let someone outside the team check a capability claim, and we work with sponsors including ONR, ARO, AFRL, and AFOSR to carry results toward operational use. Our MODIFLY platform provides scalable end-to-end multi-agent simulation for unmanned aerial vehicles, and the ModiFly team was selected for the DIU National Security Innovation Network Emerge accelerator.

Capability

Facilities and flight testing

The lab builds its own small aircraft end to end: the frame, the flight control board with onboard computing, the software stack, and the sensing modules. One team controls the compute carrier and the software, which is what makes a claim about onboard timing and autonomy measurable rather than estimated.

Openings

Join the lab

We take PhD, master’s, and undergraduate students who want to build things that fly and prove they work.

What you would work on

Students here do not pick between theory and hardware. A typical project runs from a control or learning formulation through to a flight test on an aircraft the lab built. Recent student work has appeared in Automatica, IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, the Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics, AAAI, AAMAS, RA-L, NeurIPS/ICML workshops, and the Reinforcement Learning Conference.

Where our students go

Graduates have gone to SpaceX, Raytheon, Path Robotics, Nvidia, Amazon, Dell, Bosch, NXP, General Motors, Corvid Technologies, Southwest Research Institute, and the Army Research Laboratory. Others continued to graduate school at Cornell, UC Berkeley, Rice, Texas A&M, and UT Austin, including one DoD SMART Scholarship. A former postdoc is now a faculty member at the University of Cincinnati.
